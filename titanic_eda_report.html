Section 05

Key Findings & Insights

1

Gender is the Strongest Survival Predictor

Female passengers survived at a rate of 72% compared to only 16% for males — a 4.5× difference. The "women and children first" evacuation protocol is clearly reflected in the data, making sex the most influential single variable.

2

Passenger Class Strongly Predicts Outcome

1st-class passengers survived at 50%, 2nd class at 63%, and 3rd class at only 38%. Proximity to lifeboats and social privilege significantly affected who escaped. 3rd-class passengers formed 64% of this sample yet had the lowest survival rate.

3

Children Had Elevated Survival Chances

Passengers aged under 18 survived at a rate of 56% — higher than young adults (36%) and middle-aged passengers (30%). This aligns with priority boarding policies. However, the small sample (n=9) warrants caution in generalisation.

4

Higher Fares Correlate with Better Survival

Passengers in the premium tier (£100+) had a 50% survival rate; the high tier (£30–100) reached 60%. This is partly explained by fare being a proxy for class — wealthier passengers in 1st class were cabined closer to the upper decks and lifeboats.

5

28% of Age Records are Missing — Imputation Required

Age is missing for 14 of 50 passengers. Since age correlates with survival (children prioritised), any predictive model must handle this carefully — mean/median imputation or model-based methods should be applied before training.

6

Cherbourg Passengers Showed Higher Survival

Passengers embarking at Cherbourg (C) survived at 58%, versus Southampton (S) at 33% and Queenstown (Q) at 63%. Cherbourg passengers were predominantly 1st class, suggesting embarkation port is a proxy for wealth rather than an independent factor.
